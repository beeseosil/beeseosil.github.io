Nutrient Solution

I'm using commercial nutrient solutions. I have three different kinds: two water-soluble powder sets (A and B), and a liquid set. I recommend to use a liquid one first as it's easier to mix, and the powder set is for industrial use with an EC (Electrical Conductivity) of 3.0+.

EC is the measure of the nutrient concentration in the water. Recommended EC for leafy greens is 1.2 to 2.2, which is highly dependent to the kind of crop. EC for seedlings should be 0.8 to 1.2.

Nutrient solution should be changed in every two weeks, or when the water level is too low. In a Kratky method, the water level will reduce, and the EC will increase. The air gap between water and net pots will expand. It is important to adjust the water level to prevent root suffocation.

Tap Water and RO Water

Tap water contains a good amount of nutrient. Using tap water is recommended as a baseline and to reduce the cost. The initial EC of my tap water is 0.4. Using tap water can be problematic if the mineral composition is too high (EC 0.8+).

RO (Reverse Osmosis) water: I'm using an ultrasonic humidifier with RO water. It is a good source of water but is hard to obtain. RO water is recommended if your tap water is too hard (EC 0.8+). You can also use a water dispenser or a water purifier for a small scale setup.

Water Problems

Dissolved Oxygen (DO): Low DO is a major problem in summer, as the roots will be suffocated. DO reduces when the temperature is high. The solution is to use a pump/air stone to increase the DO. This is detrimental to the roots by water evaporation.

Water Temperature: Water temperature should be 18 to 22 celsius. Nutrient solution temperature above 25 celsius is harmful to the plants. I have to use a water chiller in summer.

pH

A plant can grow well in a wide range of pH (5.5 to 7.0). An EC of 1.5+ will lead to a good pH range around 6.0.

There's no consistent result that initial pH out of the appropriate pH 6.0 plus or minus is good for yield or growth.

You can use phosporous acid, sulfuric acid, nitric acid, for NaOH for adjusting pH if the water is out of the appropriate range. I have concentrated acids but didn't used yet.

There're contradictory results for using H2O2. This can be used as a sanitizer and DO generator. While clearly increases DO for a time, some articles suggest that it's detrimental to a root without reducing algae.

In a fact, DO cannot be remain higher than a nominal value of the water unless a temperature goes down. You don't have to do any explict water treatments (sanitization for tap water, H2O2, aeration) without a observed status, or value. If such had worked, it would have been in the guidelines, but it wasn't.
