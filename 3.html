Seedling

Germination is an important step. Soak seeds in water for a few hours. This can be beneficial for the germination rate.

I'm using rockwool. Rockwool is a synthetic mineral fiber. This is a soil-like matrix that absorbs water well and provides a good support for the plant. You can also use sponges.

Germination temperature is a key factor. The optimal temperature for most leafy greens is 20-25 celsius. Lower temperature can lead to a retarded germination.

After germination, put the seeding sponges in a container with a small amount of tap water. The water level should be kept low to encourage root growth. You can cover the container with a plastic film to increase humidity.

Do not expose the seeds to light during germination. Put the seeding sponges with a radicle or sprout to a bright space. Sacrifice the most recent or smallest, damaged sprouts.

Seedling quality is critical. A epigenetic variety of crop plant seeds is not so many. A weak sprout or seedling is outstandingly stunted than normal ones, and never catch up the shape. Don't hesitate to sacrifice those. Or you may have those as a back-up.

Transplant the square sponge to a pot sponge when the sprout has a true leaf more than 1. Do not pull off the sporuts from seeding sponges. The square sponge should go with the sprout. It's good to transplant as soon as possible if the sprout has a true leaf with a propagated root.

Any seedlings or sporuts not in the container cannot be neglected. Sprouts easily dies when dry. Roots can be entangled. Watching sprouts with such a condition takes a time and it's good to have the same time point of germination.
