I suggest to seeding rather than transplanting seedlings to avoid damaging root.

It's a hard labor that transplanting grown seedlings to hydroponic pots. There's risk to damage a root of seedling. Using sponage seeding array or square-shaped sponge to seeding is preferrable.

There's a recommendation and brief evidence that priming seed can lead faster, more germination.

Based on the article, a specific tomato seed has explicitly better germination rate with a nitrate priming. I tried the method with unmatched conditions (tomato strains, substance type, morality, consistently high temperature) and result was a germination rate lower than 70%. Do a simple water priming unless such a procedure followed strictly.

Soak a sponage with tap water. Place several seeds in the shallow center of each sponge to ensure longer root propagation.

With thick closure containers and sponge-only hydroponic pots, if the root isn't long enough to touch the surface of water, sprouts cannot be removed to the container, and have to be exposured to an unfavorable environment with dry risk, high temperature, root interference, and algae. You may have 'root propagation' nursery box with a shallow container.

Don't use a paper or pulp-based culture if you don't have a genuine alternative. I have a root or waist rot experience with those.

Now place seeded sponges in a container with closure for high humidity. Use tap water for an initial setup. Place it to bright or dark space per the plant. Set a smartphone stopwatch, and check the seedings per 3-4 day from 3 days later. If shaded, do not open the container or shade frequently - increase an interval.

The Cornell hydroponics guidelines recommends add a nutrient water of 1.2 EC for a spinach. The perilla hydroponics guidelines from Korea agricultural division says using a nutrient water for seeding is detrimental.

For roughly 3 months of my expereience, leafy greens were good with tap water until they have several real leafs. Tomatos and hot peppers were also good with .6~1. EC after they have radicles.

Remove seeding sponges with a radicle or sprout to a bright space. Sacrifice the most recent or smallest, damaged sprouts.

Seedling quality is critical. A epigenetic variety of crop plant seeds is not so many. A weak sprout or seedling is outstandingly stunted than normal ones, and never catch up the shape. Don't hesitate to sacrifice those. Or you may have those as a back-up.

Transplant the square sponge to a pot sponge when the sprout has a true leaf more than 1. Do not pull off the sporuts from seeding sponges. The square sponge should go with the sprout. It's good to transplant as soon as possible if the sprout has a true leaf with a propagated root.

Any seedlings or sporuts not in the container cannot be neglected. Sprouts easily dies when dry. Roots can be entangled. Watching sprouts with such a condition takes a time and it's good to have the same time point of germination.
